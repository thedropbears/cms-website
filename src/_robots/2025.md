## Overview:
The 2025 FRC game: Reefscape presented teams with 2 game pieces, ‘coral’, a piece of PVC pipe, and ‘algae’, large inflatable balls. Most teams chose the more reliable ‘pick and place’ coral element, but with our robot, Barotrauma, we took the road less traveled by, and it made all the difference. Barotrauma used multiple automated processes, such as auto alignment and aiming, as well as sturdy mechanical systems and communication made the robot as operator friendly as possible.

## Key Features:
Dual camera localization: Two camera turrets picked up localization ‘AprilTags’, essential for automated algae intake and ballistics, if the robot doesn’t know where it is, the robot cannot perform these processes
Ground and reef intake: Algae started on a structure in the middle of each alliance’s half of the field, and had to be removed before coral could be scored. Other teams would often leave the algae on the floor, so we decided that we wanted to be able to reliably intake from both levels of the reef, as well as the floor.
Deep climb: In the endgame, robots were able to climb from either a deep or shallow cage. The deep cage was hanging from a flexible chain, which made it challenging to engage with, without displacing the cage.



## Climber mechanism:

Our climber mechanism allowed for the cage to pass through it one way, and lock in place afterwards. Large guiding flaps would drop down alongside the hook deploying. When the climber was deployed, the robot would lock its orientation towards the closest cage, reducing the amount of driver input necessary for a successful climb.

## Intake: 
The intake was able to intake from both the reef and the floor, with auto alignment on the reef, using AprilTags. It would communicate to the driver that it was aligned with the reef through LED lights that changed from red to yellow, and finally green when it was ready to drive on.

## Shooter:
The shooter was able to adjust its power and aim based on its position and angle to the barge (scoring area). It used the same LED lights as the intake to communicate with the driver, red for out of range, yellow for in range, but wrong orientation, and green for shoot.
