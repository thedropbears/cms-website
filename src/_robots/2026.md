## Southern Cross Regional Alliance Finish: 2nd Place
During playoffs, the robot adapted into a more defensive role, using its mobility and drivetrain agility to pressure opposing alliances and support alliance strategy throughout elimination matches.

## Key Features & Innovations
**Dynamic Turret Localisation**

One of the robot’s key systems was its vision assisted turret localisation. Using AprilTag tracking and PhotonVision camera systems, the robot continuously estimated its field position and automatically repositioned the turret for scoring alignment.
The turret featured approximately 300 degrees of rotation, allowing for flexible targeting and improved driver awareness during matches.

**Snow Ploughing System**

The robot was originally designed around a high speed “snow ploughing” system intended to rapidly collect and immediately launch game pieces with minimal delay. The goal of this strategy was to quickly redistribute game pieces across the field while also helping alliance partners collect balls without needing to travel deep into the neutral zone.
The combination of the over the bumper intake, spindexer, and turret assembly aimed to support fast offensive cycles and continuous gameplay pressure. However, developing a fully reliable intake and game piece handling system proved to be one of the team’s biggest engineering challenges throughout the season.

## Drivetrain & Mobility
**Swerve Drive System**

The robot utilised a swerve drivetrain, allowing for agile movement and precise control around the field. The drivetrain enabled quick directional changes, efficient positioning, and effective defensive play during elimination matches.
The combination of swerve mobility and automated turret alignment allowed the robot to remain flexible and adaptable throughout competition.

## Mechanisms
**Over-the-Bumper Intake**

The robot featured an over the bumper intake system designed for aggressive, high speed game piece collection. Multiple intake iterations and prototypes were tested throughout the season in an effort to improve consistency and collection reliability.

**Spindexer System**

A compact spindexer mechanism managed the movement of game pieces within the robot and fed them into the shooter system to support rapid cycling.

**Endgame Climber**

The robot also included a climbing mechanism developed during the season. While offensive gameplay remained the team’s primary focus, the climber provided additional strategic flexibility during endgame periods.

## Programming & Electrical Systems
The programming and electrical subsystems played a major role in the robot’s overall functionality. Vision processing, localisation systems, sensor integration, and driver communication features were continually refined throughout the build season.
Custom LED indicators provided drivers with important robot status information during matches, including blind spot awareness, robot operating states, and other real time system feedback. The integration between software and mechanical systems became one of the robot’s strongest features throughout the season.
